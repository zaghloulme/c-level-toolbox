# Crisis Communications

## Core Principle

IN A CRISIS, SPEED AND HONESTY WIN. A FAST, HONEST, IMPERFECT RESPONSE BEATS A SLOW, POLISHED, DEFENSIVE ONE EVERY TIME.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Assess the Situation

Ask:
1. What happened? (the factual situation)
2. Who is affected? (customers, employees, public, partners)
3. Is it still ongoing or resolved?
4. What is the current public exposure? (contained, spreading, viral)
5. What has been said publicly so far?

**Minimum needed: question 1.**

### Step 2: Determine Crisis Level

| Level | Description | Response Time | Example |
|-------|-------------|--------------|---------|
| **Level 1** | Minor complaint, limited exposure | 24 hours | Single bad review, minor product issue |
| **Level 2** | Growing attention, multiple stakeholders | 4-8 hours | Social media thread gaining traction, service outage |
| **Level 3** | Viral/media attention, significant impact | 1-2 hours | Data breach, major product recall, viral PR disaster |

### Step 3: Draft Response Messages

For each stakeholder group, write:

1. **Public statement** (social media, website) — Under 150 words
2. **Customer email** — Personalized, empathetic, action-oriented
3. **Internal team message** — Facts, talking points, what to say if asked
4. **Media statement** (if needed) — Factual, no speculation
5. **Follow-up update** — Sent when situation is resolved

**The Response Formula:**
1. Acknowledge what happened (no minimizing)
2. Express genuine empathy
3. State what you are doing about it
4. Commit to a follow-up timeline

### Step 4: Build Escalation Protocol

Define who does what:
- **First responder:** Who drafts the initial response?
- **Approval:** Who approves before publishing?
- **Monitor:** Who tracks social media and press coverage?
- **Follow-up:** Who sends the resolution update?

### Step 5: Post-Crisis Review

After resolution:
1. What happened and why?
2. How fast was the response?
3. What worked and what did not?
4. What systems need to change to prevent recurrence?

## Examples

### Example 1: Product Quality Issue

**Situation:** A batch of products caused adverse reactions. 15 customers reported issues on social media.

**Public Statement:**

> We have received reports that a recent batch of our [Product] caused reactions for some customers. We take this seriously.
>
> We have immediately pulled the affected batch (Lot #2847). If you purchased [Product] in the last 30 days, please stop using it and check the lot number.
>
> If you are affected:
> → Email care@brand.com for an immediate full refund
> → We will cover any related medical costs
>
> We are working with our manufacturer to identify the cause and will share a full update within 48 hours.
>
> We are sorry. Your trust matters more than any product.

**Customer Email to Affected Buyers:**

> Subject: Important safety notice about your recent order
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We are reaching out because you recently purchased [Product], and we have identified a quality issue with a specific batch.
>
> Please stop using it and check the lot number. If it reads Lot #2847, discard it.
>
> What we are doing for you:
> - Full refund processed automatically — you will see it in 3-5 business days
> - Replacement from a verified batch shipped free when available
> - If you experienced any adverse reaction, reply and we will cover your costs
>
> We are investigating with our manufacturer and will share a transparent update within 48 hours.
>
> [Name], [Title]

### Example 2: Service Outage

**Public Statement:**

> We are experiencing a service outage affecting all users. Our team identified the issue at 2:15 PM and is actively working on a fix.
>
> Current status: Data is safe. No data loss has occurred.
> Estimated resolution: Within 2 hours.
> Next update: 4:00 PM.
>
> We know you depend on us and we are sorry for the disruption. Updates posted here every 30 minutes.

**Internal Team Message:**

> Team — we have a Level 2 outage. What you need to know:
>
> What happened: [Technical explanation]
> Customer impact: All users unable to access the platform since 2:15 PM
> ETA to fix: ~2 hours
>
> If customers contact you:
> - Acknowledge the outage and apologize
> - Direct them to status.ourapp.com for live updates
> - Do NOT speculate on the cause or give technical details
> - Do NOT promise a specific resolution time beyond "within a few hours"
> - Log every customer interaction for follow-up
>
> Do not post on personal social media about this.

## Recovery & Fallbacks

- **Urge to ignore the crisis:** This always makes it worse. Even a short acknowledgment beats silence. Silence reads as guilt or indifference.
- **Legal concerns about admitting fault:** Express empathy without legal admission: "We are sorry this happened" rather than "We are sorry we caused this." When in doubt, consult legal before publishing.
- **Crisis escalating faster than response prep:** Post a brief holding statement: "We are aware of [issue] and are investigating. We will share a full update within [timeframe]." This buys time without silence.
- **Emotional or reactive drafts:** The response must be calm and empathetic regardless. Draft it, wait 15 minutes, re-read, then publish.

## Constraints

- **NEVER lie, minimize, or shift blame** — honesty is the only viable crisis strategy
- **NEVER delete negative comments** unless they contain threats, slurs, or personal information
- Response must include a specific follow-up timeline ("within 48 hours" not "soon")
- Always separate what you know from what you are still investigating
- Every public statement must include a way for affected people to reach you directly
- Internal messaging must include clear "what to say / what NOT to say" guidance
