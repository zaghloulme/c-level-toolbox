# Annual Business Review

## What this skill produces

**This skill produces a year-end business review compiled from the user's actual data.** Year-over-year metrics, milestone timeline, wins and lessons, strategic goals — every figure and event must come from the user or from files they point to.

**Never fabricate a fictional company's review.** If no data has been provided, first ask the user for their metrics and milestones, or ask for a file path. Alternatively, offer to produce a clearly-labelled sample review using representative defaults for a mid-sized B2B software company (~250 people, ~$40M revenue) that the user can then adapt.

## Core Principle

EVERY NUMBER TELLS A STORY — DO NOT LIST METRICS WITHOUT EXPLAINING WHAT THEY MEAN FOR THE BUSINESS GOING FORWARD.

## Step 1: Understand the Business Year

Gather the following before writing anything.

### Required Information (minimum 5 data points)

1. **Organization name** and what it does
2. **Year being reviewed**
3. **Key metrics** — ask for whichever apply:
   - Revenue (total, MRR/ARR, by division or segment)
   - Customer/client count and retention rate
   - Headcount changes
   - Products, services, or markets launched
   - Key operational metrics (utilization, NPS, delivery time)
4. **Top 3-5 wins** — what went well and why
5. **Biggest 2-3 challenges** — what did not go as planned
6. **Major milestones** — launches, partnerships, hires, restructuring, expansions
7. **Last year's goals** (if they exist) — what were they targeting?
8. **Financial summary** — revenue, margin, and cost trends if available

### Review Audience

Ask who the review is for:

| Type | Audience | Tone | Focus |
|------|----------|------|-------|
| Internal leadership | Senior team | Honest, strategic | Lessons + next-year priorities |
| Board/investors | Board members, shareholders | Professional, metrics-forward | Growth trajectory + returns |
| All-hands | Full organization | Direct, motivating | Wins + priorities + team role |
| External | Clients, partners, press | Narrative, selective | Story + milestones + direction |

Default: Internal leadership review.

**GATE: Do not proceed without at least 5 concrete data points about the year.**

## Step 2: Analyze and Organize

Structure the review into seven sections. Apply the "So What?" test to every item — if a data point does not connect to a lesson, a trend, or a forward-looking decision, cut it or reframe it.

### Section Breakdown

**1. Executive Summary**
Three sentences maximum: what the organization accomplished, the single biggest result, and the primary focus for next year.

Example:
> In 2025, Meridian Advisory grew from 28 to 41 staff and expanded into two new market segments. Revenue reached $14.8M, up 31% year-on-year, with net margin improving from 18% to 23%. In 2026, the priority is deepening the enterprise client base and reducing revenue concentration in the top three accounts.

**2. By the Numbers**
Metrics table with year-over-year comparison where available.

Example:

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|--------|------|------|--------|
| Revenue | $11.3M | $14.8M | +31% |
| Gross margin | 54% | 58% | +4pp |
| Net margin | 18% | 23% | +5pp |
| Active clients | 32 | 44 | +38% |
| Headcount | 28 | 41 | +46% |
| Revenue per employee | $403K | $361K | -10% |

When prior-year data is not available, present current-year numbers without fabricating comparisons.

**3. Top Wins**
List 3-5 highlights. For each win, include what happened, why it mattered, and what it unlocked.

Example:
> **Closed first enterprise contract above $1M ARR (Q3)**
> Signed a 3-year managed services agreement with a regional bank at $1.2M/year. This validates the enterprise go-to-market motion built over 18 months and shifts average contract value from $380K to $450K. The relationship also opens two warm referrals to peer institutions.

**4. Challenges and Lessons**
List 2-4 challenges. For each, include what happened, the root cause, and what changes next.

Example:
> **Headcount scaled faster than delivery infrastructure (Q2-Q3)**
> Added 11 people in 6 months before onboarding systems and team lead structure were ready. Result: utilization rate dropped from 82% to 71% for two quarters. Lesson: hiring waves above 3 people in a month require a dedicated onboarding lead, not ad hoc absorption. Structure now in place for next growth phase.

**5. Key Milestones**
Timeline format:

| Month | Milestone |
|-------|-----------|
| February | Launched enterprise pricing tier |
| April | Completed ISO 27001 certification |
| June | Opened second office location |
| August | Closed $1.2M ARR enterprise contract |
| October | Promoted 3 senior consultants to team lead |
| December | Crossed $14M ARR |

**6. Goals Review**
Compare last year's targets to actuals:

| Goal | Target | Actual | Status |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $13M | $14.8M | Exceeded |
| Gross margin | 55% | 58% | Exceeded |
| Enterprise clients (>$500K ARR) | 5 | 4 | Missed |
| New market entry | 2 markets | 2 markets | Hit |

If no prior goals were set, note this and move on.

**7. Next Year Priorities**
Define 3-5 strategic goals. Each requires a measurable target, rationale, and first action.

## Step 3: Present the Review

### Document Structure

```
# [Organization Name] — [Year] Annual Review

## Executive Summary

## By the Numbers

## Top Wins

## Challenges and Lessons

## Key Milestones

## Goals Review

## Next Year Priorities
```

### Tone by Audience

- **Internal leadership:** Unsparing. Include margin detail, headcount costs, and missed targets. Emphasize lessons over narrative.
- **Board/investors:** Metrics-forward. Frame challenges as "areas of focus." Include forward-looking financial targets.
- **All-hands:** Celebratory but clear. Connect next-year priorities to each function's role.
- **External:** Selective. Highlight milestones and direction. Omit internal metrics.

**GATE: Present the full review for confirmation before saving.**

## Step 4: Offer Follow-Up Outputs

After the review is confirmed, offer:

1. **Goals Document** — extract Next Year Priorities as a standalone file for use throughout the year
2. **Board Presentation Outline** — slide-by-slide structure for the annual board review
3. **All-Hands Talking Points** — narrative version for the leadership team kickoff
4. **Investor Summary** — one-page version with metrics table and strategic direction

Only offer options relevant to the chosen audience.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Do not invent metrics.** If a number was not provided, leave it out.
- **Do not skip the Challenges section.** A review without honest challenges is a vanity document.
- **Do not set goals without measurable targets.** "Grow the business" is not a goal. "$18M revenue by December" is.
- **Do not compare to industry benchmarks** unless explicitly requested.
- **Do not include metrics that fail the "So What?" test.** If a number does not connect to a lesson or action, cut it.
- **Do not combine multiple years into one review.** Each review covers exactly one year.

## Recovery

- **Fewer than 5 data points provided:** Ask 3 targeted questions before proceeding. If still insufficient, build around what is available and flag the gaps.
- **No prior-year data for comparison:** Present current year as the baseline. Note: "Next year's review will include year-over-year comparisons."
- **Leadership says nothing went wrong:** Push back: "What took longer than expected? What would you change with hindsight?" If they decline, note in the review that no significant challenges were reported.
- **Review for a year not yet complete:** Label as "Year-to-Date Review (through [Month])." Note that final figures will be updated at year-end.
- **Conflicting data provided:** Flag the inconsistency before proceeding and ask for clarification.
