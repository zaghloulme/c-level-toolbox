# Exit Interview Template

## Core Principle

EXIT INTERVIEWS ARE THE MOST HONEST FEEDBACK YOU WILL EVER GET — ASK THE RIGHT QUESTIONS AND ACTUALLY USE THE ANSWERS TO FIX PROBLEMS.

## Phase 1: Brief

### Required Inputs

| Input | What to Ask | Default |
|-------|------------|---------|
| **Format** | "Live interview, written survey, or both?" | Live interview with written option |
| **Interviewer** | "Who will conduct the interview? (HR, a neutral senior leader, third party)" | HR or neutral senior leader — never the direct manager |
| **Organization size** | "How many employees?" | No default — must be provided |
| **Known retention issues** | "Are there recurring reasons people leave?" | Unknown — gathering data |
| **Confidentiality** | "Will responses be shared with the departing employee's manager?" | Aggregated themes only, not attributed |
| **Departing role** | "What role is the person leaving?" | General template |

**GATE: Confirm brief before proceeding.**

## Phase 2: Design

### Question Categories

Build the interview from these categories:

1. **Role and Responsibilities** — was the job what they expected?
2. **Management and Leadership** — relationship with direct manager and senior leadership
3. **Growth and Development** — learning, promotion, career path
4. **Culture and Environment** — team dynamics, inclusion, workload
5. **Compensation and Benefits** — pay, total package relative to market
6. **Reason for Leaving** — the direct question with follow-up probes
7. **Recommendations** — what would they change?

### Question Design Rules

- Open-ended questions over yes/no — "What would have made you stay?" not "Were you happy?"
- Maximum 15 questions for a 30-minute interview
- Include 2-3 scale questions (1-10) for trackable data across multiple exits
- End with a forward-looking question that closes the relationship positively
- Never ask questions designed to guilt or retain the departing employee
- The direct manager of the departing employee should never conduct the interview

**GATE: Present the question list and wait for approval.**

## Phase 3: Build

### Deliverables

**1. Interview Question Template**
- Complete question list organized by category
- Follow-up probes for each question
- Interviewer notes on what to listen for

**2. Written Survey Version**
- Same questions adapted for self-completion
- Rating scales added where appropriate
- Estimated completion time: 10-15 minutes

**3. Interviewer Guide**
- How to set the tone (appreciative, not defensive)
- Active listening notes
- What NOT to do (do not make counter-offers during exit interviews; do not become defensive)
- Note-taking template for consistent documentation

**4. Data Tracking Sheet**
- Log responses across multiple exit interviews
- Theme categorization for pattern identification
- Quarterly summary template for HR and leadership

## Phase 4: Analysis Framework

### Pattern Recognition (after 5+ exits)

Analyze for:
- Top 3 recurring themes in reasons for leaving
- Manager-specific patterns — are departures concentrated under one leader?
- Compensation mention frequency — signals pay competitiveness issues
- Actionable recommendations appearing multiple times

### Retention Action Template

Convert exit interview data into a 3-column action plan:

| Finding | Action | Owner and Deadline |
|---------|--------|--------------------|
| [What the data shows] | [Specific change to make] | [Who does it and by when] |

## Example: Full Live Interview (30 minutes, 12 questions)

Key questions:
- "What prompted you to start looking for a new role?"
- "On a scale of 1-10, how supported did you feel by your manager? What would have raised that number?"
- "Was there a moment when you considered staying? What would have changed your decision?"
- "If you could change one thing about working here, what would it be?"
- "Would you recommend this organization to someone in your field? Why or why not?"
- "Is there anything you wish you had known before joining?"

## Anti-Patterns

- **Manager conducts their own report's exit interview** — the departing employee will not be honest. Always use a neutral party (HR, a senior peer, or an external facilitator).
- **Collecting data without acting on it** — if three people cite the same issue and nothing changes, exit interviews become theater.
- **Interrogation tone** — this is not an investigation. Create psychological safety. The organization needs honest answers more than a polished one.
- **Only asking about the negative** — include "What did you value most about working here?" to get a balanced picture and maintain goodwill.
- **Skipping exits for shorter-tenure employees** — early departures often signal onboarding or expectation-setting failures. These are among the most useful exit conversations.

## Recovery

- **Employee declines the interview:** Offer the written survey as a lower-pressure alternative. Respect their decision if they decline both.
- **Employee focuses entirely on one person:** Document specifically, probe with "Can you give me an example?" to distinguish legitimate management failure from a personality conflict. Escalate if a pattern of departure relates to one leader.
- **First few exits — no patterns yet:** Start tracking immediately. Patterns emerge after 5-10 data points. Retroactively analyzing exit themes is nearly impossible without early documentation.
- **Departing employee asks to rescind resignation:** Thank them and redirect to the hiring manager for a separate retention conversation. The exit interview is not the place for counter-offers.
