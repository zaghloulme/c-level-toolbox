# Regulatory Compliance Checklist

## Core Principle

COMPLIANCE IS NOT OPTIONAL — IT IS THE COST OF DOING BUSINESS IN A REGULATED ENVIRONMENT. PROACTIVE COMPLIANCE IS CHEAPER THAN REACTIVE PENALTIES.

## Phase 1: Organization and Industry Profile

### Required Inputs

| Input | What to Ask | Default |
|-------|------------|---------|
| **Industry** | "What industry? (financial services, healthcare, technology, manufacturing, retail, logistics)" | No default — must be provided |
| **Business model** | "What does the organization sell or provide?" | No default — must be provided |
| **Jurisdictions** | "Where is the organization registered and where does it operate?" | No default — must be provided |
| **Customer type** | "Who are the customers? (consumers, businesses, government)" | B2B |
| **Data collected** | "What personal or sensitive data does the organization collect and process?" | No default — must be provided |
| **Team size** | "How many employees?" | No default — must be provided |

**GATE: Do not proceed without industry, business model, and jurisdictions.**

## Phase 2: Universal Compliance

These apply to most organizations. Check all that apply.

```
## Universal Business Compliance

### Corporate Registration and Governance
- [ ] Legal entity properly registered with relevant authority
- [ ] Annual filings and statutory returns current
- [ ] Board and shareholder resolutions documented
- [ ] Registered address and contact details current
- [ ] Commercial registration renewed

### Tax Compliance
- [ ] Corporate income tax obligations identified and filed
- [ ] VAT registration and periodic filings current
- [ ] Withholding tax obligations on payments to contractors and suppliers
- [ ] Transfer pricing documentation (if intercompany transactions)
- [ ] Tax residency confirmed in each operating jurisdiction

### Employment and Labor
- [ ] Employment contracts in place for all staff
- [ ] Social insurance and benefits contributions current
- [ ] Worker classification correct (employee vs. contractor)
- [ ] Anti-discrimination and equal opportunity policies in place
- [ ] Occupational health and safety compliance
- [ ] Minimum wage and overtime compliance

### Data Protection and Privacy
- [ ] Privacy policy published and accessible
- [ ] Data processing register maintained
- [ ] Consent mechanisms for personal data collection
- [ ] Data breach response plan documented and tested
- [ ] Third-party data processor agreements in place
- [ ] Cross-border data transfer mechanisms documented (if applicable)

### Consumer and Commercial
- [ ] Terms and conditions published
- [ ] Refund and dispute resolution policy clearly posted
- [ ] Advertising and marketing claims truthful and substantiated
- [ ] Contracts reviewed for governing law and jurisdiction clauses

### Insurance
- [ ] General liability insurance
- [ ] Professional indemnity / errors and omissions
- [ ] Directors and officers (D&O) liability
- [ ] Cyber liability (if handling sensitive data at scale)
- [ ] Product liability (if manufacturing or distributing physical goods)
```

## Example: Mid-Size Technology Company (B2B SaaS, 150 employees, EU and Egypt operations)

**Key compliance areas:** Commercial registration in Egypt, EU GDPR (data processing agreements, breach notification procedures), Egypt Personal Data Protection Law obligations, employment contracts under Egyptian Labor Law, VAT registration and quarterly filings, information security certification (ISO 27001 in progress), professional indemnity insurance, software license compliance for third-party components.

**Gaps identified:** No formal data processing register, no documented data breach response procedure, transfer mechanism for EU customer data not formalized.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Treating compliance as a one-time task** — regulations change. Review compliance at least annually and after any material change in operations.
- **Copying another organization's policies** — their obligations differ. Build from the specific regulatory profile of this organization.
- **Assuming registration in one jurisdiction covers all** — many obligations vary by country, region, or industry segment.
- **No documentation** — if it cannot be proven, it does not count. Regulators require evidence, not assurances.
- **Waiting for enforcement** — fines, penalties, and reputational damage from reactive compliance far exceed the cost of building it proactively.

## Recovery

- **Discovered a compliance gap:** Address immediately. Document the gap, the remediation steps, and the completion date. Proactive remediation is viewed favorably by regulators.
- **Received a regulatory notice:** Involve legal counsel immediately. Respond within the specified timeframe. Do not ignore it.
- **Operating without a required license:** Apply immediately. Most regulatory bodies prefer voluntary compliance over enforcement action.
- **No compliance documentation exists:** Start documenting now. Create a compliance register and build records going forward.
