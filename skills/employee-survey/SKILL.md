# Employee Survey

## Core Principle

EVERY SURVEY QUESTION MUST BE ACTIONABLE — IF YOU CANNOT CHANGE SOMETHING BASED ON THE ANSWER, DO NOT ASK THE QUESTION.

## Phase 1: Brief

### Required Inputs

| Input | What to Ask | Default |
|-------|------------|---------|
| **Survey type** | "Full engagement survey, pulse check, or topic-specific?" | Pulse check (10-15 questions) |
| **Team size** | "How many people will take this survey?" | No default — must be provided |
| **Focus areas** | "What do you most want to learn? (satisfaction, management, culture, growth, compensation)" | Overall satisfaction and culture |
| **Anonymity level** | "Fully anonymous, anonymous with demographics, or named?" | Fully anonymous |
| **Frequency** | "One-time or recurring? If recurring, how often?" | Quarterly |
| **Distribution method** | "Which platform will you use? (Microsoft Forms, Google Forms, Typeform, SurveyMonkey)" | Microsoft Forms |

**GATE: Confirm brief before proceeding.**

## Phase 2: Design

### Question Category Framework

Build the survey from these categories, selecting based on the focus areas:

1. **Overall Satisfaction** — general engagement and likelihood to recommend
2. **Management and Leadership** — communication, support, trust in direct manager and senior leadership
3. **Growth and Development** — learning opportunities, career path clarity, promotion fairness
4. **Culture and Belonging** — inclusion, team dynamics, values alignment
5. **Workload and Wellbeing** — stress levels, sustainable pace, burnout risk
6. **Compensation and Benefits** — pay satisfaction relative to market and contribution
7. **Tools and Environment** — systems, workspace, technology

### Question Design Rules

- Mix rating scales (1-5 Likert) with 2-3 open-ended questions
- Keep total survey under 15 minutes (max 25 questions for full survey, 10 for pulse check)
- Group questions by category with clear section headers
- Include one eNPS question: "How likely are you to recommend this organization as a place to work?" (0-10)
- End with one open-ended: "What is the one change that would most improve your experience here?"
- For teams under 10 people, remove all demographic filters — responses become identifiable

**GATE: Present the question list and wait for approval.**

## Phase 3: Build

### Deliverables

**1. Complete Survey Document**
- All questions with response options formatted for the chosen platform
- Section introductions explaining why each category is being assessed
- Estimated completion time noted at the top

**2. Launch Message**
- Why the survey exists and what will happen with the results
- Anonymity assurance with specific details on how data is protected
- Deadline and estimated time to complete

**3. Reminder Message**
- Sent at the halfway point before the deadline
- Restate anonymity and purpose

**4. Results Analysis Framework**
- How to calculate scores per category
- Benchmarks: what scores indicate healthy vs. concerning (by category)
- Priority matrix: high importance + low score = act first

## Phase 4: Action Planning

### Action Planning Template

Turn results into action:

1. **Top 3 Strengths** — celebrate and protect these; they are why people stay
2. **Top 3 Improvement Areas** — pick one to act on immediately
3. **Quick Wins** — changes implementable within 2 weeks
4. **Longer-Term Initiatives** — changes requiring 1-3 months and resource commitment
5. **Communication Plan** — share results and action plan with the team within 2 weeks of survey close

### Results Communication Template

Share results with the organization including: what scored well, what needs work, and specifically what the leadership team commits to doing. Vague acknowledgment without specific action plans accelerates disengagement.

## Example: Quarterly Pulse Check (10 questions, 25-person team)

- 6 Likert scale questions across satisfaction, management quality, and workload
- 1 eNPS question
- 3 open-ended questions (best aspect of working here, biggest frustration, one change)
- Estimated time: 5 minutes
- Response rate target: 80%+

## Example: Annual Engagement Survey (25 questions, 150-person organization)

- 18 Likert scale questions across all 7 categories
- 1 eNPS question
- 3 ranking questions (benefits priorities, development preferences, communication channels)
- 3 open-ended questions
- Estimated time: 12 minutes
- Anonymous with department-level breakdowns (minimum 8 per department to preserve anonymity)

## Anti-Patterns

- **Surveying without acting** — if you collect feedback and change nothing, trust erodes faster than if you had not asked. Commit to acting on at least one finding.
- **Leading questions** — "Don't you agree our culture has improved?" biases responses. Use neutral phrasing.
- **Too many questions** — survey fatigue kills response rates. Respect the time limits above.
- **False anonymity** — if a team of 4 has department filters, responses become identifiable. Remove demographic filters for small groups.
- **Annual-only cadence** — once a year is too slow. Quarterly pulse checks surface issues before they become attrition.

## Recovery

- **Low response rates:** Shorten the survey, extend the deadline, have senior leadership personally encourage participation — without pressure.
- **Results reveal a serious concern about a specific manager:** Escalate to HR or senior leadership through a separate channel. Do not address manager-specific findings in the all-hands communication.
- **No action plan from last survey:** Acknowledge this explicitly in the launch message. Commit to a specific action timeline this round. Failure to address prior survey results is the most common cause of survey disengagement.
- **Team too small for any anonymity:** Consider external facilitation, one-on-one conversations, or an open suggestion process instead of a survey.
